# Step 5 — Conducted six validation sessions

We conducted the six sessions together with a mix of people connected to canteen ordering and food-service operations. In each session, Kruthika led the business discussion, while Lakshmi Pranathi observed how the participant used the SERVE prototype. Participants were given a task relevant to their role, and the founders noted where the workflow was clear, where the participant hesitated, and what information or reassurance they wanted. The sessions focused on practical use of the prototype rather than only asking participants whether they liked the idea.

## Participants, tasks and observations

**1. Student who orders at night**

> Task: Browse the menu, place an order and check its status.
>
> Observation: The student understood the ordering flow but wanted to know how long the food might take.
>
> Response: “I would use it if I could see when my order is likely to be ready.”

**2. Student who often visits the canteen**

> Task: Find an item, change the cart and place an order.
>
> Observation: The menu was easy to follow, but item availability needs to be clear before checkout.
>
> Response: “It would be frustrating to pay and then find out the item is unavailable.”

**3. Cashier**

> Task: Open the payment queue and confirm or reject a simulated payment claim.
>
> Observation: The cashier needs the amount and order details visible together to check a claim quickly.
>
> Response: “I need to identify the correct order quickly when several students are waiting.”

**4. Kitchen staff member**

> Task: Open a new order and move it through Preparing and Ready.
>
> Observation: A clear queue and easy-to-read item quantities matter more than extra dashboard details.
>
> Response: “The screen should make the next order obvious when the kitchen is busy.”

**5. Canteen supervisor**

> Task: Review incoming orders and pause or resume ordering.
>
> Observation: The pause control is useful, but staff need to know clearly when ordering has stopped and resumed.
>
> Response: “I would want the team to see clearly when the canteen is paused.”

**6. Food-business owner**

> Task: Review the ordering workflow and discuss how it might fit their operation.
>
> Observation: A business would first want to understand setup effort, staff training and how payments are verified.
>
> Response: “I would need to see how this fits our current counter process before deciding.”

## What we learned from the sessions

Three repeated themes emerged. First, students want useful order-readiness information and reliable item availability before checkout. This suggests that showing whether an item is available and giving a helpful indication of readiness could make the student experience more predictable. Second, staff want a queue that is easy to read, with the important order details visible at a glance. Third, supervisors need a clear pause/resume process so the whole team knows when ordering has stopped or started again. The food-business owner also highlighted that setup effort, staff training, payment verification and fit with the existing counter process would need to be understood before considering adoption.

These findings give us specific areas to review in the product: order-readiness information, item-availability visibility, queue readability and clear canteen status. They also show that a business discussion must cover operational fit and payment handling, not only the student-facing app. The sessions provide qualitative feedback from six different roles.

**Step 7 — Swap demo**

The swap demo checks whether both founders can explain and demonstrate SERVE beyond their usual roles. Kruthika presented the product, while Lakshmi Pranathi answered the business questions. The goal is for both founders to understand the product and its current position well enough to take over the other person’s part without stepping in for each other.

## Kruthika’s contribution

Kruthika presented SERVE by signing in as a student, browsing the menu and placing an order. She showed the labelled test QR and explained that payment in the current demonstration is simulated, not a live payment. She then switched to the cashier screen, confirmed the simulated payment, moved the order through Preparing and Ready, and showed the student screen updating. She also paused and resumed the canteen, marked the order as Collected, and showed the admin report.

She explained the current limitations: there is no wait-time estimate or separate pickup code, pausing is manual, and real payments and deployment are not part of this demonstration.

## Lakshmi Pranathi’s contribution

Lakshmi Pranathi answered the business questions while Kruthika presented the product. She explained who might pay for SERVE, the current status of the six business leads, what users report during validation, and the likely operating costs. Hosting costs are not yet known because hosting quotes have not been obtained.

Both founders practised the handover between the product demonstration and the business discussion. The expected outcome is that they can complete the swap demo confidently, with each founder handling the other person’s part without stepping in to answer for them.
