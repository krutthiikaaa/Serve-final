Kruthika Konni, CEO  
Prepared with Lakshmi Pranathi Balusu (CTO)

SERVE lets hostel students at SRM University-AP order from the canteen on their phones and collect their food when it is ready, instead of queueing at the counter. Canteen staff get a board of paid orders, a queue for checking payment claims and a way to pause new orders when the kitchen is overloaded. The current prototype uses simulated payments; no real money moves.

# Step 5 — Conduct six validation sessions

The following six sessions represent the mix of people we spoke to. Lakshmi Pranathi and I ran each session together: I lead the business discussion, while she observed how the person used the prototype.

## 1. Student who orders at night

Task: Browse the menu, place an order and check its status.

observation: The student understands the ordering flow but wants to know how long the food may take.

response: “I would use it if I could see when my order is likely to be ready.”

## 2. Student who often visits the canteen

Task: Find an item, change the cart and place an order.

observation: The menu is easy to follow; item availability needs to be clear before checkout.

response: “It would be frustrating to pay and then find out the item is unavailable.”

## 3. Cashier

Task: Open the payment queue and confirm or reject a simulated payment claim.

observation: The cashier needs the amount and order details visible together to check a claim quickly.

response: “I need to identify the correct order quickly when several students are waiting.”

## 4. Kitchen staff member

Task: Open a new order and move it through Preparing and Ready.

observation: A clear queue and easy-to-read item quantities matter more than extra dashboard details.

response: “The screen should make the next order obvious when the kitchen is busy.”

## 5. Canteen supervisor

Task: Review incoming orders and pause or resume ordering.

observation: The pause control is useful, but staff need to know when ordering has stopped and resumed.

response: “I would want the team to see clearly when the canteen is paused.”

## 6. Food-business owner

Task: Review the ordering workflow and discuss how it might fit their operation.

observation: A business would first want to understand setup effort, staff training and how payments are verified.

response: “I would need to see how this fits our current counter process before deciding.”

## What the sessions suggest

Across this exercise, three themes appear: students want a useful indication of readiness and reliable item availability; staff want a queue that is quick to read; and supervisors want a clear pause/resume process..

the owner asks to understand setup and operating costs before considering either option.

# Step 6 — Update the pitch and the carry-forward register

## Pitch update 

SERVE is a campus ordering and pickup platform designed to reduce uncertainty and queues around canteen orders. Students place orders through the app, while staff use a shared dashboard to review payment claims and manage preparation. This highlights three areas : order readiness information, clear item availability and a simple staff queue. The current demo uses simulated payments and is not a live payment service.

# Step 7 — My part in the swap demo

In the swap demo I will present SERVE, while Lakshmi Pranathi answers the business questions. I will sign in as a student, browse the menu, place an order, show the labelled test QR and explain that payment is simulated. I will then switch to the cashier screen, confirm the simulated payment, move the order through Preparing and Ready, and show the student screen updating. I will also pause and resume the canteen, mark the order Collected and show the admin report.

I will finish by explaining the current limitations: there is no wait-time estimate or separate pickup code, pausing is manual, and real payments and deployment are not part of this demonstration. I will rehearse using separate student, staff and admin windows until I can complete the demo twice without stopping.
